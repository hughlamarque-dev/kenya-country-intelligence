# Kenya donor intelligence: evidence and programme-design review

Reviewed 17 September 2026. Historical IATI extraction remains dated 15 September 2026.

The atlas supports a first-stage partner assessment: identify organisations with relevant experience, inspect their projects and documented financing, and check an official route for approaching them. It does not establish that a partner has money available for a new proposal. A credible approach still needs a current country-strategy fit, eligible applicant, suitable financing instrument and confirmation of the relevant programme team or call.

## What this stage establishes

Six research streams reviewed 61 organisations or institutional groups, followed by cross-review of consequential amounts, project matches and application routes. After consolidating three duplicate awards, the review retains 100 new Kenya-specific monetary references and 24 contextual records. The 26 previously documented Japanese Embassy awards remain available once.

The new references link to 81 existing project records. Of these, 76 previously had neither an IATI financial match nor a documented grant amount. Overall, 1,155 of the 2,594 researched projects now have some linked financial or documentary amount evidence; 1,439 still do not. These are evidence-coverage counts, not counts of fully financed or currently active projects. Some references cover a parent programme or a specific component.

This is a staged review. The original inventory contains 621 organisation labels, including implementing organisations, consortia and intermediaries. Reviewing 61 partner profiles does not mean every project associated with those organisations has been checked. The full queue and unresolved searches are retained.

## Financial integrity

- Keep approved loans, grant awards, budgets, commitments, receipts and expenditure separate. New references never increase the historic IATI-flow totals.
- Preserve original currencies. There is no combined monetary total across reference records.
- Keep national project amounts distinct from county allocations. County project presence does not establish the amount spent there.
- Exclude unallocated regional/global envelopes and pending financing proposals from established Kenya amount coverage.
- Retain dates and the exact project phase. Programme duration, commitment year and payment year can differ.
- Avoid assuming cash receipt from a contribution announcement. UNDP's reported contributions retain an explicit unverified cash-status label.
- Do not interpret missing evidence as zero funding. Sources can be unavailable, ambiguous or disclose only aggregate totals.

Three UNOPS activities previously treated as 100% Kenya describe global or regional programmes without a documented Kenya share. The current financial display quarantines their three annual expenditure rows, USD6,116,157, while preserving the original source trail. This does not imply zero benefit or administrative spending in Kenya. Other administrative-support activities remain flagged for review rather than being removed without sufficient evidence.

Cross-review also separated Gavi routine/multi-age HPV payments from product-switch payments, corrected the proposed IFC mobilisation component, distinguished EU financing years from implementation dates, and consolidated the same Netherlands-funded WFP/UNESCO award found by two streams. Source sections explicitly labelled for internal use were omitted from public references despite being publicly hosted.

The final description screen also found a World Bank Kenya education activity carrying a China agriculture abstract and an Irish Aid Kenya activity carrying Ukraine bakery text. The education objective and sector are corrected against an official implementation report. The Irish activity's contradictory description is withheld and its purpose remains unresolved. Verified project/country identity and financial amounts are preserved; neither case justifies removing the funding.

## Changes to the donor page

- Funding and evidence: searchable, paginated documentary records, original amounts, financial definitions, instruments, source dates, qualifications and links.
- Partners and routes: reviewed roles, application routes, eligibility where published, restricted/invitation routes and verification dates. Historical awards are not shown as open opportunities.
- Project details: non-IATI documentary evidence appears alongside existing financial matches, including the earlier Japanese awards.
- Shortlists: export the access route and source trail with saved partners. All historical partner cards are accessible rather than stopping at 48.
- Coverage: show missing-sector information and the remaining research gaps. In the default 2023–2026 donor-disbursement selection, USD3,424,927,918.95 of USD11,482,012,723.07 (29.83%) lacks a specific principal-sector classification.
- Interpretation: reported publisher flows can still overlap across financing chains and are not a consolidated national net-aid total. The current year is incomplete.

## How a programme designer should use it

1. Choose the problem, sector and county. Read the associated project evidence to identify established delivery models and partners.
2. Compare recent financial history using one measure at a time. Inspect unspecified sectors before concluding that a sector receives little support.
3. Check Partners and routes. Distinguish grantmakers, development banks, pooled funds and implementing organisations. A sovereign loan requires a different approach from an NGO grant.
4. Open the primary source. Check the recipient, phase, instrument, current strategy and eligibility. A completed programme can show relevant experience but does not prove a replacement budget exists.
5. Export a shortlist and add the proposed programme's fit, eligibility evidence, intended counterpart and next verification step. Contacting a partner and checking live funding availability remain human follow-up tasks.

## Remaining priorities

The evidence register records failed searches and blockers, including unavailable grant databases, undisclosed donor shares, ambiguous currency labels and unallocated regional funding. Further work should prioritise organisations with many unmatched projects, grant-level records beneath aggregate packages, systematic alias/consortium reconciliation, and the remaining organisation queue. County allocations and cross-publisher funding-chain deduplication require source-specific investigation; they cannot be reconstructed defensibly from mapped project locations alone.

The result is useful for landscape analysis and preparing a grounded partner approach. It is not a complete funding-market census, an assessment of proposal success probability, or a live grants calendar.
